dollar-cost averaging: 6 things to know

Dollar-cost averaging
does it work?

1

What DCA means

DCA means investing the same dollars on a schedule — like $833 each month for 12 months to deploy $10,000 — not all at once. You buy fewer shares when high, more when low.

Spreading cash

  • You have $10k today
  • $833/mo for 12 months
  • Cash sits out → cash drag
vs

Investing from income

  • You invest each paycheck
  • No cash held back
  • No drag — ideal DCA
2

The 68% rule

Vanguard and Schwab tested rolling 12-month periods from the 1920s onward. Lump sum beat 12-month DCA about 68% — because markets rise more often than they fall.

5

When DCA helps vs hurts

DCA helps when you'd otherwise stay in cash from fear, or when markets drop after. It hurts whenever markets rise while cash waits.

DCA helps

  • You'd stay in cash otherwise
  • Softens worst outcomes
  • Wins ~32% when markets fall
vs

DCA hurts

  • Markets rise more often
  • ~10% nominal left behind
  • Cash drag every up year
6

Use it right

Automate paycheck investing on payday — no drag. For windfalls, pick a short window and stick to it. Park waiting cash in yield and check your target, not the entry price.

✳ tip —
Windfall? Invest promptly or DCA 3–6 months max. Salary? Invest each paycheck — that's drag-free DCA.
That's dollar-cost averaging.
