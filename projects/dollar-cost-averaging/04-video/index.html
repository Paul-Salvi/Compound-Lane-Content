dollar-cost averaging: 6 things to know

dollar-cost averaging
does it work?

1

What DCA Means

DCA means investing the same dollars on a schedule — like $833 each month for 12 months to deploy $10,000 — not all at once. You buy fewer shares when prices are high and more when low, smoothing your average cost.

SPREADING CASH

  • You have $10k today
  • $833/mo for 12 months
  • Cash sits out → cash drag
vs

INVESTING FROM INCOME

  • You invest each paycheck
  • No cash held back
  • No drag — ideal DCA
2

The 68% Rule

Vanguard and Schwab tested rolling 12-month periods from the 1920s onward. Lump sum beat 12-month DCA about two-thirds of the time — about 68% — because markets rise more often than they fall.

$10,000 Head-to-Head

At a steady 7% for illustration, $10,000 invested day one grows to ~$10,700 after 12 months. DCA at end-of-month grows to ~$10,327. The ~$373 gap is not a fee — it is cash drag from money waiting in cash.

5

When DCA Helps vs Hurts

DCA helps when you would otherwise stay in cash from fear, or when markets drop right after — less was invested before the fall. It hurts whenever markets rise while cash waits; that ~10% nominal return is the persistent cost.

DCA HELPS

  • You'd stay in cash otherwise
  • Softens worst outcomes
  • Wins ~32% when markets fall
vs

DCA HURTS

  • Markets rise more often
  • ~10% nominal left behind
  • Cash drag every up year
6

Use It Right

Automate paycheck investing on payday — no drag. For windfalls, pick a short window (immediate to 6 months) and stick to it. Park waiting cash in yield and check your target, not the entry price.

TIP
Windfall? Invest promptly or DCA 3–6 months max. Salary? Invest each paycheck — that's drag-free DCA.
That's dollar-cost averaging.
