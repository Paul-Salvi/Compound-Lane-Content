Why Index Funds First

An index fund holds every stock (or bond) in a market index in one ticker. S&P 500 funds own all 500 large US companies at once. You get instant diversification for the price of one trade — no stock-picking, no fund-manager guesses, low fees (0.03% for VOO).

What $1,000 Can Do

The S&P 500 has returned roughly 10% annually on average since 1928 — about 7% after inflation. $1,000 at 7% real return grows to about <b>$1,970 in 10 years</b> and $3,870 in 20 years (hypothetical, constant return). Not retirement money, but a real start. The point is the habit, not the headline number.

Ups And Downs

Stocks don't go straight up. The S&P 500 has been <b>positive in about 73% of calendar years</b> since 1928 — so down roughly one in four. In any given year you might see +25% (2023, 2024) or −37% (2008), −18% (2022). The longer you hold, the more often good years outweigh bad ones. That's the deal.

The 5-Step Plan

(1) Open a brokerage (Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard). (2) Link your bank. (3) Deposit $1,000. (4) Buy VOO or FXAIX. (5) Set up an automatic monthly transfer — even $50 — to keep the habit. The first $1,000 is the hardest. The 100th is easy.

Don't Time The Market

Time in the market beats timing the market. The S&P 500 has been <b>positive in about 73% of calendar years</b> since 1928. The long-run return comes from staying invested through the bad years, not from waiting for the 'right' moment — most people who try to time end up missing the early-rebound days that drive the return.
