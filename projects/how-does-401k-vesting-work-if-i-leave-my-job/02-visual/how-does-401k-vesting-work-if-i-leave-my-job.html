1

Two Kinds Of Money

A 401(k) holds two kinds of money. Your own contributions are always 100% vested — the employer can never forfeit them. Employer contributions (the match) can be on a vesting schedule you earn over time. You see a total balance, but only part of it may be yours yet.

EMPLOYER MATCH

2

Cliff Vs Graded

Federal law caps how slow a plan can vest you. Cliff vesting: 0% for 2 years, then 100% at year 3. Graded vesting: 20% per year from year 2, fully vested at year 6. Many employers vest faster — some immediately. Check your plan document.

The Schedule At A Glance

At year 1, both schedules show 0%. Cliff hits 100% at year 3. Graded climbs 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, 100% across years 2 through 6. After that the match is fully yours. You're also 100% vested at normal retirement age or if the plan is terminated.

4

Year Of Service

A year of service is 1,000 hours worked in 12 months — roughly 20 hours a week. Part-time and seasonal work can stretch the vesting timeline. Two part-time years might not equal two vesting years, which can delay your vesting date by a full year.

TIP
A year of service = 1,000 hours in 12 months (~20 hrs/week). Part-time and seasonal work stretch the clock. Some plans only count full years — check your plan's definition.
5

Cost Of Leaving Early

Example: $60K salary, you contribute 6% ($3,600), employer matches 50% = $1,800/year. Over 2.5 years on a cliff schedule you accumulate $4,500 in match — but you're 0% vested, so leaving forfeits the entire $4,500. Not a tax penalty, just a reversion to the plan.

6

Check Before You Leave

Three steps: log into your 401(k) portal to see vested vs total balance, read the Summary Plan Description (SPD), ask HR for your vesting percentage. If you're 2 years 9 months into a 3-year cliff, waiting a few months for full vesting can be worth the conversation.

WARNING
Don't let a few months of match be the reason to stay in the wrong job. But do know the schedule — a 3-month wait for full vesting is often a real conversation; quitting at year 2 on a cliff is a $4,500 mistake.
