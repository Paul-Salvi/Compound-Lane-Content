what happens to your 401(k) when you change jobs: 6 things to know

401(k) when
you change jobs

1

Four Choices At Job Change

When you leave a job, your 401(k) goes one of four ways: (1) leave it in the old plan, (2) roll it to the new employer's plan, (3) roll it to a Rollover IRA, (4) cash out. Each has different tax, fee, and control consequences. Cash out is the worst in nearly every case.

KEEP IT GROWING

  • Leave in old plan
  • Roll to new 401(k)
  • Roll to Rollover IRA
vs

AVOID

  • Cash out
  • 10% penalty + tax
  • Up to ~30% gone
2

The Cash-Out Tax Bomb

Cashing out a 401(k) at a younger job is brutally expensive. On $20,000 vested: 22% federal tax = $4,400, plus 10% early-withdrawal penalty = $2,000, plus state tax. Total: ~$6,400 gone — and you've lost the $20K's future growth. The leftover $13,600 is what you actually get.

3

The 60-Day Rollover Trap

If you do an indirect rollover, the old plan sends you a check and you have 60 days to deposit it into a new 401(k) or IRA. Miss the deadline and the whole amount is treated as a distribution — taxed plus 10% penalty. Worse: the old plan withholds 20% for taxes upfront, and you must replace that from your own pocket.

60-DAY TRAP
Indirect rollover = 60-day deadline. Old plan withholds 20% upfront. Miss the deadline = full distribution + 10% penalty. Direct trustee-to-trustee transfer avoids both.
4

Auto-Rollovers (SECURE 2.0)

Starting in 2025, plans must auto-enroll departing employees with balances between $1,000 and $7,000 into a default IRA — unless you opt out. Under $1,000 gets force-paid as a check. Over $7,000 stays put. Auto-rolled balances must follow ERISA's safe-harbor rules.

5

Direct Rollovers: The Safe Move

Always do a trustee-to-trustee transfer: the old plan sends money straight to the new 401(k) or IRA, never to you. No 60-day clock, no 20% withholding, no tax event. Fill out the new plan's rollover form, give it to the old plan, and the money moves.

DIRECT (TRUSTEE → TRUSTEE)

  • 60-day deadline
  • 20% withheld upfront
  • Penalty if you miss
vs

INDIRECT (CHECK TO YOU)

6

Pick By Situation

Old plan has good low-cost funds? Leave it. New employer's plan is generous? Roll in. Want full control and broad fund choice? Rollover IRA at Fidelity/Schwab/Vanguard. The one wrong answer for almost everyone: cash out. The one right answer for almost everyone: direct rollover.

DEFAULT ANSWER
Default to a direct rollover. Old plan good? Leave it. New plan great? Roll in. Want control? Rollover IRA. Avoid cashing out in nearly every case.
