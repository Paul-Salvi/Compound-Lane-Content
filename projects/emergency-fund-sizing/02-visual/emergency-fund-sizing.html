emergency fund sizing: 5 things to know

Emergency fund:
how much?

1

Why not 3–6 months

The 3–6 month rule is a population average, not your number. 45% of Americans can't cover 3 months of expenses — flat for two years. Your target depends on job type, income stability, and dependents. One formula, four answers.

⚠ warning —
45% of Americans can't cover 3 months of expenses (Fed SHED 2025, published 2026). 30% can't cover it by any means.
2

The formula

Emergency fund = essential monthly expenses × target months. Essentials only: rent, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments, transportation. Not your income. Not your total spending. The cheapest correct number wins.

Wrong base

  • Uses gross income
  • Includes dining + subscriptions
  • Inflates the target
vs

Right base

  • Essentials only
  • Rent, food, insurance, transit
  • Smaller fund, same safety
3

Pick your months

FDIC baseline is 3–6 months. Dual income, both stable, low fixed costs → 3 months. Single income or dependents → 6 months. Self-employed, commission, or contract → 9–12 months (above FDIC baseline). Add 1–2 months for heavy fixed costs or a niche role.

4

Where to park it

High-yield savings, not checking, not investing. Today's top HYSAs pay 4.20–4.50% APY (up to 5.00% intro at some banks) — a real yield, FDIC-insured, liquid in 1 day. PCE inflation is 2.7% (Fed FOMC, March 2026) — your fund actually grows above inflation while it sits.

Checking / investing

  • Checking pays ~0%
  • Investing can drop 30%+ a year
  • Both defeat the purpose
vs

High-yield savings

  • 4.20–4.50% APY in 2026
  • FDIC-insured, 1-day liquidity
  • Beats 2.7% PCE inflation
5

Build it in 3 steps

Step 1: Save one month of essentials first — your starter fund. Step 2: Automate weekly transfers to a separate HYSA. Step 3: Once the starter is hit, raise contributions until you reach your target. Don't invest it. Don't touch it for lifestyle.
