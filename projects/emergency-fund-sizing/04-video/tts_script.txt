# Why the 3–6 month rule is wrong — and what to use instead.
Here's the catch.
The three to six month rule is a population average — not your number.

Your target depends on your job, your income, and your dependents.

# concept2
# The formula: essentials, not income.
Emergency fund equals essential monthly expenses, times target months.
Essentials only — rent, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt, transit.
Not your income. Not your total spending.
The cheapest correct number wins.

# concept3
# Pick your months by job type.
F-D-I-C baseline is three to six months.
Dual income, both stable — three months.
Single income or dependents — six months.
Self-employed, commission, or contract — nine to twelve months.

# concept4
# Where to park it.
High-yield savings. Not checking. Not investing.
Today's top accounts pay four point two to four point five percent A-P-Y.
P-C-E inflation is two point seven percent.
Your fund actually grows above inflation while it waits.

# concept5
# How to build it.
Step one — save one month of essentials first.
Step two — automate weekly transfers to a separate account.
Step three — raise contributions until you reach your target.
Don't invest it. Don't touch it for lifestyle.
