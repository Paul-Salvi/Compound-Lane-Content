DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Two of the most tax-advantaged accounts in the U.S. tax code, side by side.

The Roth IRA (per IRC §408A):
- Tax-free on the way in (after-tax contributions)
- Tax-free on growth
- Tax-free on the way out (qualified withdrawals)
Two distinct tax events avoided: contribution and distribution.

The HSA (per IRC §223):
- Tax-free on the way in (above-the-line deduction on Form 8889)
- Tax-free on growth
- Tax-free on the way out for qualified medical expenses
Three distinct tax events avoided: contribution, growth, and qualified medical distribution. Plus: contributions through payroll dodge FICA — a 7.65% bonus (6.2% Social Security + 1.45% Medicare) that the Roth cannot match.

The after-65 rule. The HSA's triple-tax-free edge does not last forever. After 65, non-medical withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income — same as a traditional IRA (per IRC §223(f)(2)). The account still avoids the 20% penalty that applies to non-qualified withdrawals before 65, but the triple-tax benefit is gone.

The Roth, by contrast, has no age cutoff. Qualified withdrawals are tax-free at any age, for any reason. Once you've held the Roth for 5 years and you're 59½, every dollar comes out tax-free.

The account-priority order:
1. HSA up to the annual limit — triple tax-free, plus FICA dodge.
2. Roth IRA up to $7,500 — double tax-free, no income limit issues for the Roth 401(k).
3. Roth 401(k) up to $24,500 — same tax treatment, more room.
4. Traditional 401(k) — last because the deduction is good but the future tax is uncertain.

The bottom line: HSA first, Roth second. If you have an HDHP, you're leaving real money on the table by skipping the HSA.
