Income too high? Backdoor.

High earners hit the Roth phaseout — direct contributions close. Single above one sixty-eight, married filing jointly above two fifty-two.

The backdoor Roth is a two-step workaround. After-tax money into a traditional IRA, non-deductible — then convert to a Roth. The conversion is tax-free because you already paid the tax.

The gotcha is the pro-rata rule. Other pre-tax IRA money on a $252,000 income? The IRS aggregates it. The conversion becomes partly taxable in proportion to your pre-tax balance.

Zero pre-tax IRAs elsewhere — the backdoor is clean. Save this before your next raise.
