DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Most people don't realize: the choice between Roth and Traditional IRA has a second-order effect on Social Security taxation. The Roth shield can save you thousands per year in retirement.

The rule (IRC §86):
- Provisional income = other income + 50% of SS benefits + tax-exempt interest.
- Below $34,000 single / $44,000 MFJ: 0% of SS taxable.
- Above $34,000 / $44,000: up to 50% of SS taxable.
- Above $44,000 / $64,000: up to 85% of SS taxable.
- Roth IRA withdrawals are NOT in the formula. Trad IRA withdrawals ARE.

The gap:
- Same $50,000 withdrawal at age 67.
- Trad IRA: $50K adds to provisional income. Up to $42,500 of your SS becomes taxable. Effective marginal tax: ~22% on the SS portion = $9,350 extra.
- Roth IRA: $50K doesn't count. Your SS stays as low-taxed as possible. Effective tax on the withdrawal: $0.

The strategy:
- Goal in early retirement: keep provisional income under $34K single / $44K MFJ.
- Use Roth for the dollars you need to spend before age 70 (when RMDs force Trad IRA distributions).
- Save Trad IRA dollars for age 70+ when SS + RMDs push you over the threshold anyway.
- The Roth shield compounds: less taxable SS → less provisional income → lower bracket → less capital gains drag → compounding effect over 20+ years.

Common mistakes:
- Converting Trad to Roth in low-income years and then withdrawing from the Trad in early retirement — you lose the Roth shield.
- Forgetting that the SS taxation formula is joint, not individual (MFJ thresholds are different).
- Not accounting for tax-exempt municipal bond interest, which DOES count toward provisional income.

Full threshold math + the conversion ladder at compoundlane.com.

Educational content, not financial advice.
