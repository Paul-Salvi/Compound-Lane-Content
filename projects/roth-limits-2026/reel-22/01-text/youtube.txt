DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Most people know the $7,500 Roth IRA limit. Few know about the after-tax 401(k) trick that can shovel up to $72,000 a year into Roth — the mega backdoor Roth.

What it is:
- An after-tax contribution to your 401(k), then an in-plan conversion to Roth.
- Same tax-free growth as a Roth IRA — but at ~10x the annual limit.
- Bypasses the Roth IRA income phaseout entirely (no $153K / $242K cliffs).

The three things that must line up:
1. Your 401(k) plan must allow after-tax (non-Roth) contributions.
2. Your 401(k) plan must allow in-plan Roth conversions.
3. Your 401(k) plan must allow in-service rollovers (or automatic conversions).
- About 70% of large 401(k) plans support the mega backdoor. Most small plans do not.

The math (IRC §415(c) limit = $72,000 for 2025):
- Employee deferral: $24,500 (2026) — pre-tax.
- Employer match: ~$5,000-$10,000 — pre-tax.
- After-tax contribution: the remainder, up to the $72,000 cap.
- Convert the after-tax dollars to Roth weekly to minimize pre-conversion earnings.

How to start:
- Ask HR these three questions at your next open enrollment.
- If all three are yes: contribute the max, then automate weekly conversions.
- If no: you can roll after-tax 401(k) dollars to a Roth IRA — a slightly slower but equivalent path.

Common mistakes:
- Not knowing whether your plan supports after-tax contributions.
- Forgetting to convert (the after-tax dollars grow pre-tax until converted).
- Letting the after-tax dollars sit for years, then converting a large balance with significant earnings — those earnings get pro-rated (basis recovery first).

Full mechanics + the HR-question script at compoundlane.com.

Educational content, not financial advice.
