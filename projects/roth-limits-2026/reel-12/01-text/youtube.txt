DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
The 2026 Roth IRA contribution limit is $7,500 per person, per year. The 2026 Roth 401(k) limit is $24,500 — $17,000 more room, same tax-free growth (per IRS IR-2025-111 and Notice 2025-67).

The key difference: the Roth 401(k) has no income phaseout.

The Roth IRA phases out at:
- $153,000-$168,000 modified AGI for single filers
- $242,000-$252,000 modified AGI for married filing jointly
Above those ceilings, direct Roth IRA contributions are zeroed out.

The Roth 401(k) phases out at nothing. Earn $400,000, $800,000, more — you're still eligible. The phaseout is an IRA-only rule (per IRC §408A). For high earners, the Roth 401(k) is the only direct route into a Roth.

The mega backdoor angle: if your employer's 401(k) plan allows after-tax contributions and in-plan Roth conversions (per IRS Notice 2014-54), you can stack:
- $24,500 employee Roth 401(k)
- Employer match (separate cap)
- After-tax 401(k) contributions (separate bucket, plan-specific cap)
- In-plan conversion of the after-tax bucket

In the right plan, a high earner can exceed $80,000 in annual Roth savings. The mega backdoor Roth works through the 401(k) — never through an IRA.

Bottom line: the $7,500 Roth IRA is the on-ramp. The $24,500 Roth 401(k) is the highway. If you're a high earner who hasn't explored the 401(k) Roth and the mega backdoor, you're leaving tens of thousands of tax-free dollars on the table.
