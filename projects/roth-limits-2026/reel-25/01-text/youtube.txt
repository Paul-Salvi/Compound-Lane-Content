DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Most people think about the Roth IRA in 30-year terms. The 40-year math is where compounding goes from impressive to absurd.

The 40-year math ($7,500/yr, 7% real return):
- Total contributed: 40 × $7,500 = $300,000
- FV factor at year 40: ((1.07^40 - 1) / 0.07) ≈ 199.6
- End balance: ~$1,497,000
- Effective growth multiple on contributions: ~5.0×

The 30-year comparison (from Reel #8):
- Same $7,500/yr, 7% real return, 30 years
- End balance: ~$708,000
- Effective growth multiple: ~3.1×

The last decade does the work:
- From year 30 to year 40, the balance goes from $708K to $1,497K.
- That decade alone adds $789,000 — more than the entire first 30 years added combined.
- Compounding is back-loaded. The early years are just the foundation.

The trade to get to 40 years:
- Start at 22, run until 62 — 40 years uninterrupted.
- $300,000 of post-tax dollars over 40 years ($7,500 × 40).
- Pre-tax equivalent at a 25% bracket: $400,000.
- The engine: $400,000 pre-tax turns into $1,497,000 Roth = ~3.7× growth.
- In a brokerage, the same $400K (after cap-gains drag) would compound to ~$1.2M pre-tax equivalent.

Why most people stop at 30:
- Kids. Mortgages. Career changes. College. Life happens.
- The "save 30 years" Roth advice is correct — but the 10-year extension is where the magic is.

What to do:
- If you're under 30: plan for 40. The discipline now is the gift.
- If you're at 30: don't stop. Year 31-40 is the biggest chunk.
- If you're past 30 and stopped: restart. The 5-year clock resets per contribution.
