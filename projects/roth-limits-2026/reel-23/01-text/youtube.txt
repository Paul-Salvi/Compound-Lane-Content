DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Most people know the 5-year rule for earnings. Few know there are actually TWO 5-year clocks running on a Roth IRA — and confusing them triggers the 10% penalty even after 59½.

The two clocks (IRC §408A(d)):
- **Earnings 5-year rule** (§408A(d)(2)): one clock, opened when you first contributed to ANY Roth IRA. It governs qualified (tax-free) earnings withdrawals.
- **Conversion 5-year rule** (§408A(d)(4)): a NEW clock starts for EACH conversion. Withdraw converted dollars before this clock expires = 10% penalty, even past 59½.

The trap:
- Convert $50,000 in 2024, then withdraw $10,000 in 2026 (you're 62).
- You're past 59½, but the conversion clock started in 2024. Five years don't expire until Jan 1, 2030.
- 10% penalty applies to the $10,000 — even though you're 62.

The fix:
1. Convert early in the year (gives you five full calendar years before Jan 1 of the year-after-five).
2. Wait to withdraw conversions until 59½ AND the 5-year clock has expired.
3. Stack your conversions — one large conversion in a low-income year beats many small ones.

The withdrawal order (FIFO, IRC §408A(d)(4)):
1. Regular contributions (any time, tax- and penalty-free).
2. Conversions — oldest first (each one with its own 5-year clock).
3. Earnings (subject to the original 5-year rule + 59½).

Track each conversion's clock on Form 8606 Part II. The IRS doesn't do this for you.

Full mechanics + the early-year conversion strategy at compoundlane.com.
