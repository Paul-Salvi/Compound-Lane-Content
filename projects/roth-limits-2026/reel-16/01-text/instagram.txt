FULL CAPTION (300-500 chars total — Compound Lane "study notes" format):
A Roth conversion is a one-way bet on your future tax rate. Once converted, you can't undo it (per IRC §408(d)(2)).

Three numbers decide:

1. Current marginal bracket — that's the cost. Convert $50,000 at 24% = $12,000 federal tax.

2. Expected future bracket — RMDs at 73, Social Security, RSU vesting. Conversion wins if future is higher.

3. Conversion size — fill up to the top of your current bracket. Not a penny more.

A 24% bracket filer can convert ~$94,000 without spilling into 32%.

Save this before your next conversion.
