Convert? Three numbers decide.

A Roth conversion is a one-way bet on your future tax rate. The IRS treats the converted amount as ordinary income this year.

Number one: your current marginal bracket. That's the cost. Number two: your expected future bracket. RMDs at seventy-three, Social Security, RSU vesting — all push future income up.

Number three: the conversion size. Convert up to the top of your current bracket — not a penny more. A 24% bracket filer can convert $50,000 this year — that $50,000 never gets taxed again.
