DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
The 2026 Roth IRA limit is $7,500 per person, per year (per IRS IR-2025-111). Add the $1,100 catch-up at 50+ for $8,600 total. Contribute more, and the excess is hit with a 6% excise tax — every year the excess remains in the account (per IRC §4973).

The $1,000 example:
- Excess contribution: $1,000
- Annual excise tax: $1,000 × 0.06 = $60
- 5-year cumulative tax (if uncorrected): $300
- Plus: earnings on the excess accrue their own 6% until corrected

The 6% is on the excess only, not the total balance. A $1,000 excess in a $50,000 Roth triggers $60/year, not $3,000.

Two paths to fix an over-contribution:

Path 1: Recharacterize. Convert the excess contribution (plus attributable earnings) to a traditional IRA contribution. This works only if you're otherwise eligible to contribute to a traditional IRA — meaning your income doesn't exceed the deductibility phaseouts (or you accept a non-deductible contribution). Earnings on the recharacterized amount are also moved; you don't owe the 6% on them.

Path 2: Withdraw the excess. Pull the $1,000 excess plus attributable earnings out of the Roth before your tax-filing deadline (including extensions). The earnings on the excess are taxable as ordinary income for the year the contribution was made, and subject to the 10% early-withdrawal penalty if you're under 59½. No 6% excise tax.

Either way, file Form 5329. Path 1 uses Part V to report the recharacterization. Path 2 uses Part III to claim the "withdrawal of excess contribution" exception. Either filing removes the 6% obligation.

The bottom line: the 6% excise tax is a 6% per year, compounding by neglect. Correct the mistake and the tax stops. Leave the excess in for 10 years and you've paid 60% of the excess in excise tax alone.
