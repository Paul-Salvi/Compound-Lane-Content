DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
The 2026 IRA contribution limit is the same for both account types: $7,500 per person, per year (per IRS IR-2025-111). The only difference between Roth and Traditional is when you pay tax. That's the whole decision.

Traditional IRA: you contribute pre-tax dollars (deductible), the money grows tax-deferred, and you pay ordinary income tax on withdrawals in retirement. The deduction saves you your marginal tax rate today.

Roth IRA: you contribute after-tax dollars (not deductible), the money grows tax-free, and qualified withdrawals are tax-free. The trade is paying tax today at your marginal rate.

The decision rule: compare your marginal tax rate today to your expected marginal tax rate in retirement. If retirement rate is higher, Roth wins — you locked in today's lower rate. If retirement rate is lower, Traditional wins — the deduction saves you today's higher rate, and withdrawals are taxed at the lower retirement rate.

Most young workers in the 12%–24% bracket today should lean Roth — they're likely to retire in a similar or higher bracket, and the tax-free compounding on a 30-40 year horizon is massive. High earners in the 32%–35% bracket who expect to retire in a lower bracket should lean Traditional — the deduction is worth more than the tax-free withdrawal.

The math: $7,500 per year for 30 years at 7% real return is roughly $708,000 contributed. In a Traditional at 24% retirement rate, you keep $538,000 after tax. In a Roth, you keep the full $708,000. The $170,000 gap is the Roth's tax-free compounding.

Same limit, different timing. The decision is yours.
