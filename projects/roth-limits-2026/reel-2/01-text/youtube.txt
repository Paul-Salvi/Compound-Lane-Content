DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
The 2026 IRS retirement-plan limits include a Roth IRA catch-up contribution for anyone who is 50 or older. For 2026, the catch-up is $1,100. That is on top of the $7,500 base contribution limit. The total for a single eligible filer is $8,600.

The catch-up is a flat add-on, not a multiplier. You do not get 1.15 times the base — you get $7,500 plus $1,100, period. The IRS Notice that released the 2026 numbers (Notice 2025-67) and IRS News Release IR-2025-111 both confirm the $1,100 figure.

The math, lined up:
- Base contribution: $7,500
- Catch-up (50+): $1,100
- Total per eligible spouse: $8,600

If you are married, both spouses can do this — if both are 50 by year-end. The catch-up is per eligible spouse, not per household.

The one rule that decides if you qualify: you must turn 50 by December 31 of the contribution year. If you turn 50 on December 31 itself, you qualify for the full calendar year. The IRS measures the age by the end of the year, not on the day you contribute.

This is the cheapest "free money" in the IRA system. The $1,100 grows tax-free for decades. If you are 49, set a calendar reminder for your 50th birthday year and fund the catch-up on January 2.

Full notes, the contribution calculator, and the IRS-canonical sources at compoundlane.com.

Educational content, not financial advice.
