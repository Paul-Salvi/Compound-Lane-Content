FULL CAPTION (300-500 chars total — Compound Lane "study notes" format):
The pro-rata rule (IRC §408(d)(2)) makes your backdoor Roth almost entirely taxable if you have pre-tax IRA money.

The $90,000 example:
- $90,000 pre-tax traditional IRA
- $7,000 after-tax backdoor contribution
- Pro-rata pre-tax ratio: 90/97 = ~92.78%
- Of the $7,000 converted, $6,495 is taxable

The fix: roll the $90,000 pre-tax IRA into your 401(k) first (if the plan accepts rollovers). 401(k)s aren't aggregated. Pro-rata finds zero pre-tax. The full $7,000 conversion is tax-free.

File Form 8606 every year — it's the paper trail.

Save before your first backdoor.
