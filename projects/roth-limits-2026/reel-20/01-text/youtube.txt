DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Maxing the Roth is the cornerstone. But what about the dollars above the limit? The answer is usually a taxable brokerage — and the gap between the two is smaller than most people think.

The 30-year math, $7,500 a year, 7% real return:

Roth IRA:
- Total contributions: 30 × $7,500 = $225,000
- Future value: $708,000
- All tax-free on withdrawal (qualified)
- Effective tax rate: 0%

Taxable brokerage (low-turnover index fund, e.g. VOO):
- Total contributions: $225,000
- Future value: $570,000 (after capital gains tax on sale)
- Long-term capital gains rate: 15%
- Annual tax drag: ~0.5% (dividends + occasional rebalance)
- Effective tax rate on the gain: ~15%

The gap: $138,000. Significant, but not the millions that "always max your Roth" messaging implies.

Why the gap is smaller than you think:
- The long-term capital gains rate is 15%, not the 24%+ ordinary income rate.
- Low-turnover index funds minimize annual dividends.
- Buy-and-hold investors defer capital gains for decades.
- Step-up in basis at death can eliminate capital gains entirely for heirs.

When the brokerage wins:
- No contribution limit — invest any amount.
- No income phaseout — high earners can fully fund.
- No 5-year rule — no Roth conversion clock to track.
- No 10% early-withdrawal penalty — access anytime for any reason.
- No RMDs — the SECURE 2.0 changes don't apply.

The right answer: max the Roth first, then fund the brokerage with the next dollars. Both. Don't skip the brokerage because you can only max the Roth — the dollars you don't invest are the most expensive dollar you can lose.
