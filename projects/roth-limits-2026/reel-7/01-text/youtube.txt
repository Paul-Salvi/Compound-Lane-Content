DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Labor Day is more than a long weekend. It's a hidden funding window for your 2026 Roth IRA. If you're paid biweekly, your Labor Day paycheck still lands — and the Roth contribution can ride along, locking in three extra days of tax-free growth.

The 2026 Roth IRA contribution limit is $7,500 per person, per year (per IRS IR-2025-111). Add the $1,100 catch-up if you're fifty or older — total $8,600 per spouse, per year. The IRS deadline is your tax-filing deadline the following April 15. Funding early doesn't extend the deadline, but it does lock in the year's compounding from day one.

Three days of growth on $7,500 at 7% real return (the S&P 500 historical average, Damodaran 2026 update) is roughly $4.31. Small dollars, but the habit is everything. Open the account, fund it, automate the contribution, don't touch it till fifty-nine and a half. The first dollar is the hardest.

Why this matters: time in market beats timing the market. Three days of delay is a small gap, but it compounds. Over a 30-year horizon, every quarter of a year of delay costs tens of thousands of dollars. Funding today — even on a long weekend — locks in the year.

The mechanics: open a Roth at any major brokerage (Vanguard, Fidelity, Schwab — all offer commission-free Roth IRAs with low-fee index funds). Link your bank, transfer $7,500, buy VOO or a target-date fund. Set the same transfer to repeat next year. Then close the laptop and enjoy the rest of the long weekend.

Why the IRS allows you to wait until April: contribution deadline ≠ market timing. The IRS gives you until tax day to maximize the prior year's contribution, but the tax-free growth clock starts the day the cash hits the Roth. Funding early is the only free lunch.

Full notes, the brokerage checklist, and the IRS-canonical sources at compoundlane.com.
