DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Most first-year Roth mistakes are recoverable. Three aren't. Here's what to skip if you're opening a Roth at 22 (or any age).

The three mistakes:

1. Contributing over the income limit. If you earn above the phaseout ($153K-$168K single in 2026), your contribution is excess and triggers the 6% excise tax per year it sits (IRC §4973). The fix isn't skipping — it's a backdoor Roth: contribute to a Traditional IRA, then convert. Earnings grow tax-free; the excess-contribution problem goes away.

2. Parking the balance in cash or a money market. The 2026 contribution cap is $7,500. The cap on lost growth is uncapped. A dollar at 22 left to 67 at 7% real return becomes roughly $32. Parked in cash, it stays a dollar.

3. Withdrawing contributions at 23 for a trip. Roth contributions can come out any time, tax- and penalty-free. But the opportunity cost at 7% real over 40 years is roughly 5x the withdrawal. $7,500 withdrawn at 23 = $7,500 today vs ~$300,000 at 67.

The compounding math:
- $7,500 in cash for 45 years at 7% real = $1,000,000+ in growth lost.
- $7,500 in S&P 500 for 45 years at 7% real = ~$240,000 contribution balance (8x), all tax-free.

The fix:
- Step 1: Open the Roth at Fidelity, Vanguard, or Schwab. Don't optimize — pick the lowest-fee S&P 500 index fund (FXAIX, VOO, or SWPPX).
- Step 2: Contribute the full $7,500 the day you open it. Time in market beats dollar-cost averaging at this age.
- Step 3: Set dividend reinvestment on. Don't take cash distributions. The 40-year compounding engine needs every dollar working.
- Step 4: Add a calendar reminder for January 1, 2027. The deadline for 2026 contributions is April 15, 2027.

Full first-year checklist at compoundlane.com.
