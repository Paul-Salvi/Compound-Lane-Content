Wait five years? Lose millions.

Start your Roth at twenty-five — $7,500 a year, 7% real return. Start at thirty instead. The early starter contributes $187,500 over forty years. The late starter contributes $262,500 over thirty-five. Late starter puts in more — ends with less.

The five years you wait aren't just missed contributions. They're lost compounding on those contributions you never recover.

Doubling contributions later doesn't catch you up. A thirty-year-old has to contribute roughly 25% more per year to match the twenty-five-year-old by sixty-five — and still ends with about $1,600,000 less.
