DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Fall is the home stretch for Roth conversions. Here's the calendar math, the tax-bracket logic, and the form-8606 mechanics.

The 100-day window (Sept 22 → Dec 31):
- A conversion is taxed in the calendar year it happens. Convert in 2026 = 2026 income.
- The deadline isn't a specific date — it's "before midnight on December 31."
- Converting in the last week of December is the most common, and the most panic-driven.

Lock in this year's bracket:
- Estimate your 2026 taxable income now. Subtract deductions. Find your bracket.
- If you have room between your projected income and the top of your bracket, convert up to that amount.
- $50,000 converted at 22% saves ~$11,000 vs. converting at 32% in a future year (assuming the same future tax rate, before compounding).

Why fall is the right window:
- Markets often dip in October — a lower balance means fewer dollars converted to fill your bracket.
- You have a better read on your 2026 income by Q3 than you do in January.
- The 5-year conversion clock starts on January 1 of the year AFTER the conversion — converting in 2026 means the clock doesn't expire until Jan 1, 2032.

Track on Form 8606 Part II:
- Every conversion is a separate line — date, amount, basis carried over.
- The 5-year clock per conversion (IRC §408A(d)(4)) is non-waivable.
- October is the sweet spot. Earlier than you think — by December, the broker's customer-service queue is jammed.

The trap: people convert in late December, then realize they blew past their bracket. By then, it's locked. The cure is to convert early in the year, not late.

Full bracket math + Form 8606 walkthrough at compoundlane.com.

Educational content, not financial advice.
