DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Most people think December 31 is the Roth IRA contribution deadline. It's not. The actual deadline is the tax filing deadline of the following year — April 15, 2027 for 2026 contributions (IRC §219(c)). And the IRS does NOT carry forward unused contribution room.

The 92-day clock:
- Q3 ends Wed, Sept 30, 2026.
- Q4 starts Thu, Oct 1, 2026 and runs 92 days to Dec 31, 2026.
- Then Q1 2027 runs 90 more days until the April 15, 2027 deadline.
- Total runway: 197 days. But waiting until Q4 means you miss half the year's compounding.

The room you have:
- $7,500 — 2026 individual Roth limit (IRC §219(b)(5)).
- $1,000 — 2026 age-50 catch-up (IRA only — 401(k) catch-up is separate).
- $8,500 — 2026 total at age 50+ ($7,500 + $1,000 catch-up).
- $15,000 — 2026 spousal Roth if both spouses max ($7,500 × 2).
- The 6% excise tax (IRC §4973) applies to ANY excess contribution, including amounts over the cap.

The cost of waiting:
- $7,500 in your Roth on Oct 1 vs Dec 31 = 92 days of additional compounding.
- $7,500 at 7% real return for 92 days = ~$130 in real return (small but compounding).
- $7,500 in your Roth on April 15, 2027 vs Jan 1, 2026 = full year lost = ~$525 in real return.

The 3-step action:
- Step 1: Log into your Roth account today. Note the year-to-date contribution. Subtract from $7,500. That's your remaining room.
- Step 2: Fund the remaining room in one lump sum on Oct 1, 2026. Time in market beats dollar-cost averaging in Q4.
- Step 3: Set a calendar reminder for April 1, 2027 to do a final 2026 contribution if you have leftover room from a bonus or side income.
