Roth at sixty. Two clocks.

Every Roth has two clocks. Age fifty nine and a half. The five year rule. Both must be met for earnings to come out tax free.

The conversion clock. Every conversion starts its own five year clock. A conversion can't be withdrawn penalty free for five years, even past fifty nine and a half.

The cost. $50,000 conversion at age sixty, two years early: ten percent penalty. That's $5,000 gone. At sixty three: zero.

Map every conversion year. Withdraw contributions first, conversions oldest first. Touch earnings only after both clocks. Save this before you withdraw.
