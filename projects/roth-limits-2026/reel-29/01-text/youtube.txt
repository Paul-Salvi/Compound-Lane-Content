DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Most people think turning 59½ unlocks a Roth. It doesn't. Two clocks must be met for earnings to come out tax- and penalty-free. The first is age 59½. The second is the 5-year rule.

The two clocks (IRC §408A(d)):
- Clock 1 (age 59½): you've reached age 59½.
- Clock 2 (5-year rule): the Roth has been open for at least 5 tax years. The clock starts January 1 of the tax year you first contributed.
- Both met → all withdrawals (contributions, conversions, earnings) are tax- and penalty-free.
- Only Clock 1 met → contributions are still free, but earnings trigger the 10% early-withdrawal penalty (IRC §72(t)).

The conversion clock:
- Every Roth conversion starts its OWN 5-year clock. The clocks don't consolidate, don't reset, and don't disappear at 59½.
- If you converted $50,000 in 2024 and turn 60 in 2026, the conversion clock started in 2024.
- Withdraw the converted $50,000 in 2026 (age 60): only 2 years old → 10% penalty = $5,000.
- Withdraw the converted $50,000 in 2029 (age 63): 5 years old → penalty-free.

The cost:
- A $50,000 conversion withdrawn 2 years early = $5,000 penalty.
- Same $50,000 at the 5-year mark = $0 penalty.
- The 5-year clock applies per-conversion, not per-account.

The strategy at 60:
- Step 1: Identify the oldest Roth on your account list. The 5-year clock starts January 1 of the tax year you first contributed.
- Step 2: Map every conversion year. Use a spreadsheet: year, amount, clock-mature date.
- Step 3: Withdraw contributions first (always penalty-free, regardless of age or 5-year status).
- Step 4: Withdraw conversions in order of clock-maturity — oldest first.
- Step 5: Only touch earnings after both clocks are met for that specific Roth.

Full conversion clock + 5-year rule map at compoundlane.com.
