DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Form 8606 is the IRS form that tracks your IRA basis. Without it, the IRS has no record of what you put in — and treats every dollar that comes out as taxable earnings (per IRC §408(d)(1)).

What Form 8606 does:

Part I — Nondeductible traditional IRA contributions. Track the after-tax dollars you put into a traditional IRA, year by year. This basis carries forward to the year of conversion.

Part II — Roth conversions. Track the year, the amount, and the basis. This is the part that backdoor Roth filers routinely skip. Without Part II, the IRS assumes the conversion was 100% pre-tax money — and your $7,500 conversion becomes fully taxable on withdrawal.

The cost of skipping. You do a $7,500 backdoor Roth conversion in 2026 and skip Form 8606. Thirty years later, the Roth is worth $400,000. You withdraw it. Without 8606 on file, the IRS treats the entire $400,000 as taxable income. At 24% federal: $96,000 owed. The form costs nothing. The cost of skipping is six figures.

When to file. File Form 8606 every year you do a non-deductible traditional IRA contribution or a Roth conversion. Attach it to your Form 1040. There is no statute of limitations on tracking your own basis — so if you skipped prior years, file Form 1040-X (amended return) with Form 8606 for each missed year.

The 5-year rule. Form 8606 also tracks the start of your Roth's 5-year clock for the 10% penalty on converted amounts. Without it, the IRS cannot verify the clock — and the penalty applies indefinitely.

The bottom line: Form 8606 is the form that does the work. Most tax software will not generate it for you. You must add it to your return yourself.
