DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
A Roth IRA for a minor is a custodial account — the parent or guardian opens it on the teen's behalf, but the money belongs to the teen. Three numbers decide what they can put in for 2026.

Number one: earned income. The teen needs to have actually been paid — W-2 wages, tips, or self-employment income. Allowance, gifts, and investment gains don't count. The IRS Pub 590-A spells out "earned income" in detail.

Number two: the 2026 limit. The standard Roth IRA limit is $7,500 for 2026 (per IRS IR-2025-111). But the contribution can't exceed the teen's earned income. So a sixteen-year-old with a $3,000 summer job can put in $3,000. Not $7,500. Earned income is the binding cap for minors.

Number three: the year they turn eighteen. A custodial Roth converts to a regular Roth on the teen's eighteenth birthday. The five-year clock for tax-free withdrawal of earnings also starts on that date — the earlier the teen opens, the earlier the clock clears.

Why this matters: a Roth opened at sixteen with a $3,000 summer job, left untouched until sixty-five, grows to roughly $100,000+ at a 7% historical return assumption. Tax-free. Compound interest is the engine. The first dollar is the hardest — and the most valuable.

Open a custodial Roth before the teen's first W-2. The earlier, the better.
