DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
The Roth IRA contribution deadline for tax year 2025 is April 15, 2026 — the same as your federal tax return. Not September 15. (Per IRC §219(f)(3) and IRS Pub 590-A.)

Where the Sept 15 myth comes from:
- Sept 15 is the deadline for quarterly estimated tax payments (Q2 installment for the current year).
- Sept 15 is also the deadline for certain business returns — partnerships (Form 1065) and S-corporations (Form 1120-S) on extension.
- These deadlines are unrelated to IRA contributions.

The actual IRA rules, in plain English:

Rule 1: The deadline is April 15 of the year following the tax year for which you're contributing. Tax year 2025 contributions are due April 15, 2026.

Rule 2: File an extension (Form 4868) and your IRA contribution deadline extends to October 15 of the year after. This is automatic — you don't have to make a separate election for the IRA extension.

Rule 3: After October 15, the contribution is barred. There is no further extension. The IRS will not accept a 2025 contribution on October 16, 2026 or later.

Rule 4: A late contribution is treated as a contribution for the next tax year. If you try to contribute $7,500 for 2025 on October 16, 2026, the IRS treats it as a 2026 contribution. You lose the 2025 contribution room forever — it does not roll forward.

The "lose the room" trap. The 2025 contribution room of $7,500 expires when the calendar hits April 15, 2026 (or October 15, 2026 with an extension). Anything contributed after that date is a 2026 contribution, not 2025. The 2025 room is gone — and you cannot recharacterize or otherwise reclaim it.

Bottom line: the deadline is April 15 (or October 15 with an extension), not Sept 15. Set a calendar reminder for April 15, 2026 to fund your 2025 Roth.
