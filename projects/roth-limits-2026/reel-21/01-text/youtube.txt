DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
The 10% early-withdrawal penalty is one of the most-misunderstood parts of a Roth IRA. It's not a blanket rule — there are five IRS-recognized exceptions that erase the penalty entirely.

The basics (IRC §72(t)):
- The 10% penalty applies to EARNINGS only, not to your contributions.
- Your contributions can come out any time, for any reason, tax- and penalty-free.
- The penalty is on top of ordinary income tax.

The five exceptions:
1. First-time home purchase — up to $10,000 lifetime per person.
2. Qualified higher education expenses.
3. Unreimbursed medical expenses above 7.5% of AGI.
4. Health insurance premiums while unemployed.
5. Birth or adoption expense — up to $5,000 per child.

The strategy:
- Max the Roth. The penalty is what makes the tax-free growth possible.
- Stack 6-12 months of expenses in a taxable brokerage for true emergencies.
- The 5 exceptions cover most "qualified" emergencies — use them when they apply.
- The Roth is for retirement. The brokerage is for everything else.

Common mistakes:
- Pulling earnings before 59½ for non-qualified reasons and paying 10% + 24%+ tax.
- Not knowing that the first-time home buyer exception is a lifetime $10K cap.
- Forgetting that the 5-year rule still applies to earnings on conversions.

Full exceptions list + Form 5329 walkthrough at compoundlane.com.

Educational content, not financial advice.
