DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
Single filers don't get a flat "no" at a specific income. They get a sliding window. For 2026, that window is $153,000 to $168,000 of modified adjusted gross income (MAGI). Inside the window, the IRS Pub 590-A Worksheet 2-2 reduces your allowed Roth contribution. Above $168,000, the door closes — you cannot contribute directly to a Roth IRA at all. Zero.

The line that matters is MAGI, not salary. The IRS worksheet takes your AGI, adds back a few items (student-loan deduction, foreign-earned-income exclusion, and a handful of others), and that's the MAGI for Roth contribution purposes. The phase-out is computed on MAGI, not on what your W-2 says.

What if you're above the ceiling? The backdoor Roth is the workaround. Contribute to a non-deductible traditional IRA, then convert to Roth. Same end account, different on-ramp. The pro-rata rule can complicate this — that's a separate reel.

How to estimate your MAGI before year-end: take last year's AGI, add back the items on Worksheet 2-2, add expected raises and bonus income, subtract expected pre-tax 401(k) contributions. If you're inside the $15,000 window, decide how much Roth to put in before December 31. If you're above, plan the backdoor for January.

Why this matters: a Roth at $153,000 of MAGI gets a partial contribution, capped at a sliding-fraction of the $7,500 limit. A Roth at $168,000+ of MAGI gets nothing directly. Plan ahead — the IRS doesn't prorate after the fact.

Full notes, the MAGI worksheet walkthrough, and the IRS-canonical sources at compoundlane.com.
