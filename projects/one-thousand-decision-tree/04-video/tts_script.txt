# intro
You finally have a thousand dollars that isn't earmarked for rent. What do you do with it? Four steps. In order.

# concept1 — Cash first
Before you invest a dollar, ask one question. If your car died tomorrow, could you pay the bill without going into debt?

If the answer is no — keep five hundred to a thousand dollars in a high-yield savings account. Not a checking account. A real, separate, FDIC-insured account paying four to five percent.

The reason isn't the interest. It's optionality. Money you might need in six months has no business being in the market.

# concept2 — Kill debt over 8%
Credit cards charge twenty-two to twenty-eight percent. Buy now, pay later runs zero to thirty.

Every dollar you pay down on a twenty-four percent balance is a guaranteed, tax-free, risk-free twenty-four percent return. You will never — in your life — find an investment that reliably beats paying off that balance.

# concept3 — Roth IRA + S&P 500
If you have earned income and you're under fifty, a Roth IRA is the single best deal in personal finance.

Money in after-tax. Grows tax-free. Comes out tax-free in retirement. The contribution limit is seven thousand dollars a year.

You don't need to pick stocks. A low-cost S&P 500 index fund — VOO, VTSAX, or FZROX — gives you the five hundred largest US companies for an expense ratio under a tenth of a percent.

# concept4 — Rest to brokerage
Whatever's left — the final zero to five hundred dollars — goes into a regular taxable brokerage. Same S&P 500 fund. No tax break, but no contribution limit, and no penalty if you need it before fifty-nine and a half.

This is the flexible bucket. Future monthly savings live here too.

# outro
That's the thousand-dollar decision tree.

Five hundred to a thousand in cash. Kill debt over eight percent. Roth IRA plus S&P 500. The rest to a regular brokerage. Boring on purpose.
