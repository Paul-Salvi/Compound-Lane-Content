DESCRIPTION FULL (200-500 words):
You finally have $1,000 that isn't earmarked for rent. The question isn't "what's the best stock?" — it's "where does this money sit first?" Here's the order, in plain English.

Step 1 — Cash first. Before you invest a dollar, keep $500–$1,000 in a high-yield savings account. Not a checking account. A separate, FDIC-insured account paying 4–5%. The reason isn't interest — it's optionality. Money you might need in 6 months has no business being in the market.

Step 2 — Kill any debt over 8%. Credit cards charge 22–28%. Buy-now-pay-later runs 0–30%. Every dollar you pay down on a 24% balance is a guaranteed, tax-free, risk-free 24% return. You will never find an investment that reliably beats paying off that balance. Federal student loans (~5–7%) and mortgages (~6–8%) — invest instead of prepaying.

Step 3 — Roth IRA + S&P 500. If you have earned income and you're under 50, a Roth IRA is the single best deal in personal finance. Money in after-tax, grows tax-free, comes out tax-free in retirement. Contribution limit is $7,000/year (2024). A low-cost S&P 500 index fund — VOO, VTSAX, or FZROX — gives you the 500 largest US companies for an expense ratio under 0.10%.

Step 4 — Rest to a regular brokerage. Whatever's left, the final $0–$500, goes into a regular taxable brokerage. Same S&P 500 fund, no tax break, but no contribution limit and no 59½ penalty if you need it early. This is the flexible bucket. Future monthly savings live here too.

Not sexy. Not fast. The actual order.

Educational content, not financial advice.
