FULL CAPTION (300-500 chars total — Compound Lane "study notes" format):
The first $1,000 isn't about picking the best stock. It's about the order.

1. Cash first — $500–$1,000 in a high-yield savings account paying 4–5% (FDIC-insured). Optionality, not interest. Money you might need in 6 months has no business in the market.

2. Kill debt over 8%. Credit cards run 22–28%. Every dollar on a 24% balance = a guaranteed 24% return. You will never beat that with an investment.

3. Roth IRA + S&P 500. If you have earned income and you're under 50, this is the single best deal in personal finance. After-tax in, tax-free out. $7,000/year limit (2024). A low-cost index fund (VOO, VTSAX, FZROX) — expense ratio under 0.10%.

4. Whatever's left → a regular brokerage. Same S&P 500 fund, no limit, no penalty before 59½. The flexible bucket.

Not sexy. The actual order.

Educational content, not financial advice.
