the $1,000 decision tree: 4 steps

I have $1,000 —
now what?

1

Cash first

Before you invest a dollar, keep $500–$1,000 in cash in a separate high-yield savings account. The reason isn't the 4–5% interest — it's optionality. Money you might need in 6 months has no business being in the market.

Checking account

  • Pays ~0% interest
  • Easy to spend by accident
  • No FDIC cushion beyond $250k
vs

High-yield savings

  • Pays 4–5% today
  • Separate account, out of sight
  • FDIC-insured, liquid in 1 day
2

Kill debt over 8%

Credit cards charge 22–28%. BNPL runs 0–30%. Every dollar paid down on a 24% balance is a guaranteed, tax-free, risk-free 24% return. You will never find an investment that reliably beats paying off that balance.

3

Roth IRA + S&P 500

If you have earned income and you're under 50, a Roth IRA is the single best deal in personal finance. Money in after-tax, grows tax-free, comes out tax-free in retirement. Contribution limit is $7,000/year (2024). Pick a low-cost S&P 500 fund — VOO, VTSAX, or FZROX — for an expense ratio under 0.10%.

✳ tip —
Put $500 in a Roth IRA, pick a low-fee S&P 500 fund, automate monthly, don't touch for 30 years. Boring on purpose.
4

Rest to brokerage

What's left — the final $0–$500 — goes into a regular taxable brokerage. Same S&P 500 fund, no tax break, but no contribution limit and no 59½ penalty if you need it early. This is the flexible bucket. Future monthly savings live here too.
