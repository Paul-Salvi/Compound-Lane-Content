# I Have $1,000 — Now What? The 4-Step Decision Tree

> A friend who works in finance sketched this on a napkin. No jargon, no
> textbook. Just the order of operations for your first $1,000.

You finally have a thousand dollars that isn't earmarked for rent. The
question isn't "what's the best stock?" — it's "where does this money
sit first?" Here's the order, in plain English.

## 1. Keep $500–$1,000 in cash first

Before you invest a dollar, ask: if my car died tomorrow, could I pay
the bill without going into debt? If the answer is no, that $500–$1,000
stays in a high-yield savings account. Not a checking account. Not "in
my sock." A real, separate, FDIC-insured account that pays 4–5% right
now.

The reason isn't interest. It's optionality. Investing money you might
need in 6 months is a guaranteed way to sell at the worst time.

## 2. Kill any debt over 8% interest

Credit cards. Buy-now-pay-later. Store financing. If the rate is over
8% — and most credit cards are 22–28% — every dollar you pay down is a
guaranteed, tax-free, risk-free return equal to that rate. You will
never, in your life, find an investment that reliably beats 24%.

Pay the minimums on everything else. Throw every spare dollar at the
highest-rate balance until it's gone. Then the next.

## 3. Open a Roth IRA, put $500 in a low-fee S&P 500 fund

If you have earned income and you're not yet 50, a Roth IRA is the
single best deal in personal finance. Money goes in after-tax, grows
tax-free for the rest of your life, and comes out in retirement with
zero tax owed. The contribution limit is $7,000/year (2024).

You don't need to pick stocks. A low-cost S&P 500 index fund — VOO,
Vanguard's VTSAX, Fidelity's FZROX — gives you a slice of the 500
largest US companies. Expense ratio under 0.10%. Boring on purpose.

Put $500 in. Automate it. Don't touch it for 30 years.

## 4. Whatever's left, into a regular brokerage

You've now got:
- A cash buffer (no more 24% credit-card panic)
- No high-interest debt
- A started Roth IRA

The remaining $0–$500 goes into a regular taxable brokerage account.
Same S&P 500 fund. No tax break, but no contribution limit, no
penalty if you need it before 59½. It's the flexible bucket.

This is also where future monthly savings live.

---

## The napkin

1. Cash buffer first (high-yield savings).
2. Kill debt over 8% (every dollar = guaranteed return).
3. Roth IRA + S&P 500 fund (tax-free compounding for life).
4. Leftover → regular brokerage (same fund, no limits).

Not sexy. Not fast. The actual order.

*This is general education, not financial advice. Your situation may
differ — talk to a fee-only fiduciary if your taxes, debt, or income
picture is complicated.*
