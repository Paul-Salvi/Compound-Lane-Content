401(k) job change: 6 things to know

What happens to my 401(k)
if I quit?

1

Your 4 options

Your 401(k) doesn't disappear when you leave a job. Leave it, roll it to your new plan, roll it to an IRA, or cash it out — only cashing out triggers taxes.

2

Direct vs indirect rollover

A direct rollover moves money trustee-to-trustee — no tax, no deadline. An indirect rollover pays you a check with 20% withheld, and you've got 60 days to deposit the full amount.

Direct rollover

  • Money moves account-to-account
  • No tax, no penalty
  • No deadline
vs

Indirect rollover

  • Check made out to you
  • 20% withheld upfront
  • 60 days to deposit full amount
3

The 60-day trap

A check means 20% withheld for taxes — but you still have to deposit the full 100%, so you front the missing 20% yourself and get it back later.

✳ watch out —
Miss the 60-day window and the entire distribution becomes taxable, plus a 10% penalty if you're under 59½.

6

Small balances can be forced out

Under SECURE 2.0, plans can auto cash-out balances under $1,000, and auto-roll $1,000–$7,000 into a safe-harbor IRA if you don't choose. You usually get 30+ days' notice first.
