## 1. The 2026 Roth IRA landscape (numbers & rule changes worth knowing)

These are the load-bearing facts that any 2026 Roth reel can hook on. Cross-checked across IRS, Fidelity, NerdWallet, T. Rowe Price, MagnifyMoney, Guideline, Boston Private Bank.

### Contribution limits (2026)
- **Under 50:** $7,500
- **50+:** $9,000 (standard $7,500 + $1,000 catch-up)
- **2026 contribution deadline:** April 15, 2027
- **Mega backdoor Roth (401k side) under 50:** $24,500 employee + up to $72,000 total; 50–59: $32,500 + $80,000; 60–63: $35,750 + $83,250
- Sources: IRS via [Ascend Planning](https://www.ascendplanning.com/blog/irs-announcement-new-401k-and-ira-limits-for-2026), [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/investing/roth-ira-contribution-limits), [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/roth-ira-contribution-limits-for-2025-and-2026-8784948), [Fidelity Learning Center](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/ira-contribution-limits)

### Income phase-outs (2026)
- **Single / HoH:** $153,000 – $168,000
- **MFJ:** $240,000 – $257,000
- **MFS:** $0 – $10,000
- Sources: [T. Rowe Price](https://www.troweprice.com/personal-investing-advice/ira/roth-ira-income-limits.html), [MagnifyMoney](https://www.magnifymoney.com/news/investing/roth-ira-income-limits), [Guideline](https://www.guideline.com/blog/roth-ira-phase-out-ranges/)

### 2026 rule changes (real, confirmed)
- **Roth 401(k) RMDs eliminated as of 2024** under SECURE 2.0 (already in effect by 2026)
- **Super catch-up for ages 60–63:** $11,250 (vs. $7,500 regular catch-up) — workplace plans only, NOT Roth IRAs
- **SECURE Act 3.0** (proposed in Senate May 2026): starting Jan 1 2026, employees 50+ can make Roth catch-up contributions even if their base contributions are pre-tax; **starting Jan 1 2027**, employers can offer Roth matching (employees pay tax on the match up front, withdrawals are tax-free). Watch this for late-2026 content.
- Source: [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning/personal-finance/roth-ira-conversion-limits-2026), [New York Life Investments](https://www.newyorklifeinvestments.com/insights/money-muse/will-roth-iras-exist-in-2026)

## 6. Common mistakes / pain points that go viral

Cross-referenced from: [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/roth-ira-mistakes-avoid-2026-230000714.html), [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/investing/roth-ira-mistakes-to-avoid), [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/roth-ira-viral-mistake), [Buzzsprout 5 Things podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2589409/episodes/19172473-5-things-you-need-to-know-about-roth-iras-before-you-open-one), [Reddit r/personalfinance wiki](https://www.reddit.com/r/personalfinance/wiki/roth ira).

### Mistakes that consistently get clicks (in the order creators cover them)
1. **"I over-contributed to my Roth"** — excess contribution trap. 6% excise tax per year. Fix: withdraw excess + earnings (NIA) before the tax filing deadline (Oct 15 with extension, Apr 15 without), recharacterize (limited), or absorb into next year's contribution.
2. **"I withdrew earnings and got taxed"** — 5-year rule violation. Earnings on contributions withdrawn before age 59½ + 5-year rule are taxable + 10% penalty. (First-time home purchase up to $10K is the famous exception.)
3. **"I missed the deadline"** — April 15 (with extensions) is the absolute last day. Filing a tax extension does NOT extend IRA contribution deadline (common misconception).
4. **"My income was too high"** — phase-out surprise. The single most common Reddit r/personalfinance beginner confusion. Hook: "If you make over $X, the IRS quietly phases you out."
5. **"I didn't know about the income phase-out"** — same problem, different framing. Good for "they don't tell you" hook.
6. **"I put the wrong investments in my Roth"** — Roth is a wrapper, not an investment. Many beginners leave it as cash in the settlement fund. (Massive TikTok confusion point per Reddit wiki.)
7. **"I forgot about the 5-year rule"** — owns 5 separate Roth IRAs = 5 separate 5-year clocks. Per-account tracking is a known confusion.
8. **"I expected a tax break"** — Roth contributions are not deductible. Beginners coming from 401(k) mindset expect a tax break and don't get one.
9. **"My contributions showed up as taxable income"** — they aren't, but the year-of-contribution shows them on the return in a way that confuses tax software. (Real Reddit thread pattern.)
10. **"I opened it, now what?"** — Roth is just an account; you have to pick investments (index funds, target-date funds). Cash sits in a settlement fund by default.

### Reddit r/personalfinance common beginner questions (2026)
- "Should I pick Roth or Traditional?" (most-asked)
- "Is a Roth IRA a type of 401(k)?" (no — different account types)
- "Roth IRA vs. Roth 401(k)?" (Roth IRA = you open at a brokerage; Roth 401(k) = employer plan)
- "Can I contribute if I earn too much?" → backdoor Roth
- "Is my Roth IRA invested automatically?" → no, you have to pick investments
- "Do I need a 401(k) to open a Roth IRA?" → no, they're independent

### Pro-rata rule (the backdoor Roth trap)
- The most common "I did everything right but the IRS still taxed me" story
- If you have *any* pre-tax balance in *any* Traditional, SEP, or SIMPLE IRA, the IRS taxes your conversion proportionally
- Hook potential: "The backdoor Roth trick that costs high earners thousands"
